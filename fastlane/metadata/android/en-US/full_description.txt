Jargon is a voice typing keyboard that recognizes your speech <b>on your phone</b>: what you say is never uploaded. The only thing it uses the internet for is downloading a speech model, when you ask it to. It is a fork of Kaiboard by Kai, built on whisper.cpp, parakeet.cpp and transcribe.cpp.

It is a dictation strip, not a full keyboard: keep your normal keyboard for typing and flick up on Jargon's enter key to get back to it. This copy ships without a speech model, so every byte of the app is open source; on first open it offers the models to download, from a small English one to Canary 1B, which translates between 25 European languages.

<b>Dictating</b>
• Tap the microphone to start, tap again to finish. Or hold the microphone while you speak and let go to finish; drag up while holding to throw the recording away.
• Text appears only when you finish, as one piece, with the spacing and capitalization the sentence around the cursor needs.
• Say "question mark", "period" or "comma" at the start or end and the mark is typed. Say "smiley face", "facepalm" or "thumbs up" and the emoji is.
• Highlight text and dictate to replace it.

<b>Editing</b>
• Backspace deletes a character, or holds to repeat and then delete a word at a time. Drag it left to undo.
• Enter sends, searches or adds a new line, whichever the field expects. Flick it up to switch keyboard; hold it to open settings.

<b>Models and languages</b>
• Download a recommended model from the settings screen, or add your own: Whisper models (the multilingual ones understand many languages), Parakeet or Canary models in .gguf format. Only one is kept in memory at a time.
• With Canary, slide left on the microphone to pick the language to write in.
• The keyboard times itself once on your phone and picks the fastest thread count.
